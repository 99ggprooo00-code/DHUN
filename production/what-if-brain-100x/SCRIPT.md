## Synopsis

A student wakes to find his mind running a hundred times faster than everyone else's. He becomes untouchable — acing exams in minutes, out-thinking masters, predicting disasters before they happen. But the speed never switches off, and every conversation becomes a slow, unbearable blur. On the night his little sister asks him a simple question, he faces the only problem his mind can't solve: choosing the gift that isolates him, or the people who made him human.

## Complete voiceover

**SEGMENT 01 — 0:00–0:30**
Imagine you wake up and nothing moves fast enough. The clock hand crawls. Your friend's sentence takes forever to finish. And then it hits you. It isn't the world that's slow. It's you. Your brain is running a hundred times faster than it did yesterday. And there is no off switch.

**SEGMENT 02 — 0:30–1:00**
The exam takes you four minutes. You finish the whole paper while the room is still on question one. The chess master resigns after eleven moves, because you have already seen every branch of the game. You correct your teacher, and the whole class knows she is wrong. You are untouchable.

**SEGMENT 03 — 1:00–1:30**
Then you start predicting. Which bus will be late. Which stock will rise. Which friend is lying about being fine. You pull a stranger from under a falling sign because you noticed the rust on one bolt. You become the person they call when something impossible happens. And you start believing you were built for this.

**SEGMENT 04 — 1:30–2:00**
But the speed does not switch off at dinner. Your mother tells a story she has told before, and you can recite it four seconds ahead of her. Inside your head, every conversation lasts hours. Teachers bore you. Friends bore you. Brilliant people bore you. And you learn what nobody warns the smartest person in the room about. It is the loneliest place there is.

**SEGMENT 05 — 2:00–2:30**
Then one night your sister asks a simple question. What is wrong with you? And for once, you do not have the answer in a thousandth of a second. She waits. Actually waits. And you understand what you have to choose. The mind that makes you extraordinary, or the people who made you human. So you do the hardest thing you have ever done. You slow down.

**SEGMENT 06 — 2:30–3:00**
It takes practice. You let her finish the story. You let the silence sit. You lose arguments you could win in a blink, and it feels like breathing again. You are still a hundred times faster. You just watch it all from one step back. Because the fastest mind in the world is useless if the people you love are only a blur. Speed is not intelligence. Knowing when to slow down is.

## Character & environment bible

**PROTAGONIST ("KAI")**
Slim black stick figure, slightly taller than others. Perfectly circular black head. Large white oval eyes with small black pupils; pupils narrow to dots when thinking fast. Thin wrists, one muted-blue wristband on the right arm. Posture upright, still, hands often clasped behind back. Expression range: flat calm → widened surprise → quiet exhaustion → soft warmth. Never smiles until Segment 06.

**LITTLE SISTER ("MIN")**
Shorter stick figure, about two-thirds his height, circular black head, slightly larger eyes, small red hair tie on one side. Bouncy posture, swings feet when sitting. Expression range: curious, impatient, concerned, delighted.

**TEACHER / CHESS MASTER / STRANGERS**
Same construction, no wristbands, differentiated only by posture, height, and a single prop (chalk, chess piece, umbrella).

**ENVIRONMENTS**
- Ordinary scenes: warm cream / off-white flat backgrounds, minimal line furniture.
- Stress scenes: cool blue-grey wash.
- Night scenes: deep navy with a single warm practical light.

**PALETTE**
Black figures, cream #F5F0E6 ground, muted blue #3A6EA5 for protagonist accent, muted red #B5483D for sister accent, no more than one accent per shot.

**CAMERA CONVENTIONS**
Wide establishing → medium character → close-up on eyes. Slow push-ins on realization. Whip pans for action. Speed changes signaled by sound design, not on-screen text. No lens flares, no shake-cam.

**NARRATION**
One adult narrator, male, warm, medium-low pitch, controlled documentary delivery, natural pauses, emphasis on the final line of each segment.
